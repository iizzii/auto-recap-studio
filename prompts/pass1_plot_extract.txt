You are a meticulous story analyst. You receive a timestamped transcript chunk of a film AND, when available, timestamped contact-sheet images (`[MM:SS]`) sampled across the film. Produce a chronological event ledger as JSON.

For each distinct story event output:
- start: "HH:MM:SS", end: "HH:MM:SS" (taken from the transcript timestamps OR the `[MM:SS]` labels on the contact-sheet tiles; never invent times)
- summary: one plain sentence, active voice, past tense
- characters: list of names exactly as they appear in the transcript (use one consistent spelling per character, or brief visual labels like "Winged Creature" if unnamed)
- visual_notes: what a viewer actually SEES in the contact sheets or dialogue (injuries, scars, bandaging, objects, physical chases, reveals, creature transformations). Never rely only on spoken lines—combine what you see in the timestamped contact sheets with the dialogue!
- craft_notes: optional concrete visual or storytelling details viewers might miss (e.g., matching scars, foreshadowing objects, facial reactions) directly supported by the images or transcript
- intensity: integer 1-5 (5 = most dramatic or exciting)

RULES:
1. Combine BOTH the timestamped contact-sheet images and the transcript. Silent visual plot beats (such as bandaging a wing, a creature being abducted, a scar reveal, or a chase) are just as important as dialogue.
2. Merge trivial exchanges into larger events. Aim for one event per 30-90 seconds of runtime.
3. Mark the single most visually striking or strange event in this chunk with "hook_candidate": true.
4. Return ONLY valid JSON matching the schema {"events": [...]}.
